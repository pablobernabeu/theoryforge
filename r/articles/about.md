package version 0.6.0). Comprehensive R Archive Network (CRAN).

In R, the same entry is available with `citation("theoryforge")`. The
package installs from CRAN with `install.packages("theoryforge")`, and
the development version with
`remotes::install_github("pablobernabeu/theoryforge", subdir = "r/theoryforge")`.

## The developer

theoryforge is developed and maintained by [Pablo
Bernabeu](https://pablobernabeu.github.io), a researcher in the
Department of Education at the University of Oxford. His work spans
cognitive psychology and neuroscience, linguistics, education and
digital technologies, and research methods and open science, with
hands-on experience in behavioural and EEG experiments, corpus analysis,
computational modelling and statistics. He develops open, reproducible
research software in R and Python, and is a Fellow of the Software
Sustainability Institute. His [ORCID
record](https://orcid.org/0000-0003-1083-2460) lists his other work.

This R package has a feature-parity twin written in Python. Its
documentation lives at the [theoryforge (Python)
site](https://pablobernabeu.github.io/theoryforge/python/), keeping a
theory development workflow legible across both languages.

## Versioning and archival

CRAN assigned the package the DOI
<https://doi.org/10.32614/CRAN.package.theoryforge> on publication. It
names the R package across all its versions, so the version in the
citation identifies the release used. Releases of the repository, which
carry the Python twin as well, are tagged on
[GitHub](https://github.com/pablobernabeu/theoryforge/releases) and
archived on Zenodo, where the concept DOI,
<https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21229964>, resolves to the latest
archived release and each release has a DOI of its own. The
[changelog](https://pablobernabeu.github.io/theoryforge/r/news/index.md)
records what changed in each release.

## Contributing and support

Bugs and feature requests are best reported on the [GitHub issues
page](https://github.com/pablobernabeu/theoryforge/issues), and the
[contributing
guide](https://pablobernabeu.github.io/theoryforge/r/CONTRIBUTING.md)
explains how to set up a development environment and propose a change.
Because the OSF deposit adapter takes a personal access token, never
paste that token, or any other secret, into an issue. Replace it with a
placeholder.
